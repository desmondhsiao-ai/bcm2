About BCM2

BCM2

Ask questions by ordinary text message — no data connection needed — and keep every answer filed here on your phone.

  1. Write your question here and tap “Text it”. Your Messages app opens with the text ready — press Send. That one hop into Messages is unavoidable on a phone; BCM2 makes everything either side of it one tap.
  2. The reply SMS carries a short answer plus a tappable link, https://desmondhsiao-ai.github.io/bcm2/?tag=general&ref=A47314. In Messages it turns blue — tap it and BCM2 opens straight at that answer.
  3. No data when you tap? The short answer is already there in the text itself, readable in Messages with no connection at all. Once you are in BCM2, paste the whole reply into “File a Reply” and BCM2 reads the link — the new https link or the older sms2ai://tag/REF — files the answer under its group, and matches it to your waiting question.

Put BCM2 on your Home Screen

  1. Safari (iPhone): tap the Share button, then “Add to Home Screen”, then “Add”.
  2. Chrome (Android): tap the ⋮ menu, then “Add to Home screen”.
  3. After that, tap the blue carton icon to open BCM2 like an app. Your saved questions live in this browser on this device, so keep using the same browser you added it from.

Both styles work here and in “File a Reply”: the new https link above, or the older sms2ai://general/A47314.

Honest limits: BCM2 cannot read your Messages inbox or send a text by itself — no web app can. Tapping an https link in a text needs a data connection to load this page; the answer in the text itself needs none, and once BCM2 is open, reading old answers, drafting questions and filing replies — including pasting an older sms2ai:// link — all work with no signal at all. Only the text itself travels, over the ordinary phone network. This static copy keeps everything on this one device and browser — it does not sync to your other devices.

With “Automatic” chosen, the group is the first word of your text, lowercased — the same rule your Make.com scenario uses. A question with no spaces (many languages write without them) files under #general. Or pick a group yourself: general, casual, work, idea, soul, study, translation, or one of your own. Answers come back in British English, within 120 characters, with their source named.
